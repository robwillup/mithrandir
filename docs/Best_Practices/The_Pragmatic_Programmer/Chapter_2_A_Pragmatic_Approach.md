# A Pragmatic approach

> Knowledge isn't stable. It changes rapidly. Your understanding of requirements may change following a meeting with 
> the client. The government changes a regulation and some business logic gets outdated. Tests may show that the chosen
> algorithm won't work. All this instability means that we spend a large part of our time in maintenance mode,
> reorganizing and reexpressing the knowledge in our systems.
>
> Maintenance is part of the entire development process.
>
> The only way to develop software reliably is to follow DRY principle:
>
> *Every piece of knowledge must have a single, unambiguous*
> 
> Why do we call it DRY? Don't Repeat Yourself.

## How does duplication happen?

- Imposed duplication: developers feel they have no choise - the environment imposes duplication
- Inadvertend duplication: Developers don't realize that they are duplicating info.
- Impatient duplication: Developers get lazy and duplicate because it seems easier.
- Interdeveloper duplication: Multiple people working on the same thing.
